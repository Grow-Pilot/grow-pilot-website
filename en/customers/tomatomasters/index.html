01 · How it went

Speed known, quality not recorded

Tomato Masters grows tomatoes and peppers on 21.5 hectares in Deinze, Belgium, and works with Priva. Path registration shows how fast each worker is working.

How well the work was done was not recorded in a structured way. There was no fixed system for it, so checks were not done consistently on the same points. Who needed to be checked again, and when, lived mostly in the supervisor's head.

02 · What they were looking for

Quickly knowing who handles the crop well

Because of Belgian regulations, employment contracts are relatively short. For Tomato Masters it is therefore essential to know quickly who has the skills to handle the crop well. That means staying close to the work from the start, with a way of checking that fits how the greenhouse actually runs. Counting where counting makes sense, a score from 1 to 5 or yes/no where that fits better. The result: far more sight of where the problem points are.

In time they want to reward workers for the quality they deliver, through the bonus module. The advantage of GrowPilot already running at several greenhouses: no need to invent everything themselves, but start with what other growers have already brought in and sharpen from there.

03 · The pilot

What the pilot involves

The pilot starts on 5.6 hectares of lit tomatoes, on top of the existing Priva registration. The check points were set up based on what works at other growers and went through point by point together with Tomato Masters and adjusted where needed.

Two supervisors check all workers every week, in two rounds. A deviation triggers a re-check within an adjustable time frame. Management does spot checks, so they also know how the checking is done.

With a lot of changing staff, I want to know from day one whether the work is being done the way it should. That is grip: having the overview, and staying right on top of it.
